# Flemish Network - User Guide

This guide explains the current local interface: **Network**, **Collections**, **Grow**, and **Settings**. The screenshots show the information currently available in the workspace.

## Access, roles, and navigation

**Purpose:** provide secure access to the Flemish Network workspace.

The sign-in page is the entry point for staff. After signing in, the main navigation gives access to the directory, collections, research tools, and settings.

1. **Enter your details.** Type the email address that has access and its password.
2. **Start your session.** Select [[Sign In]].
3. **Open a main area.** Select [[Network]], [[Collections]], [[Grow]], or [[Settings]] in the top navigation.
4. **Open your account.** Select your name to go to [[My Account]]. Select the exit icon to sign out.
5. **Use the functions available to your role.** Viewer can read; Editor can also change records and use Grow; Admin can also manage access and system settings.

- **Role:** the level of access assigned to a staff account: Viewer, Editor, or Admin.

## Networks / Map

**Purpose:** explore where people and organizations in the network are located.

The map places records at their known locations. You can search, filter the network, inspect a place, and open the profiles shown there.

1. **Open the map.** Select [[Network]] and then [[Map]].
2. **Search the network.** Enter a name or a description in the search field and select [[Submit search]].
3. **Choose what is shown.** Open [[Filters]] and select People, Organizations, scope, sector, occupation, or Flemish connection.
4. **Inspect a location.** Select a marker or numbered cluster, then select a record to open its profile.
5. **Adjust the view.** Use the zoom, map theme, and [[Reset View]] controls.
6. **Clear your selection.** Remove a filter chip or select [[Reset All Filters]].

- **Map marker:** one location on the map. A numbered marker groups several records at or near the same location.

## Networks / List

**Purpose:** browse the network as a searchable directory.

The list shows people and organizations as cards. From this page you can open profiles, export results, or add one or more records to a collection.

1. **Open the directory.** Select [[Network]] and then [[List]].
2. **Narrow the results.** Use the search field and [[Filters]].
3. **Show or hide a group.** Select [[People]] or [[Organizations]] to expand or collapse it.
4. **Open a profile.** Select a person or organization card.
5. **Save records together.** Select [[Add to collection]] on one card or [[Add all to collection]] for the displayed group.
6. **Download the results.** Select [[Export]].

- **Network record:** a person or organization that has been added to the main directory and can appear in Map, List, and Stats.

## Networks / Stats

**Purpose:** understand the size and composition of the network.

Stats summarizes the records currently included in your search and filters. It shows totals and groups the results by occupation, sector, Flemish connection, and location.

1. **Open the summary.** Select [[Network]] and then [[Stats]].
2. **Read the totals.** Review the numbers for People, Organizations, and Cities.
3. **Review each category.** Check Occupations, Sectors, Flemish Connections, and Locations.
4. **Open a group.** Select a category or its [[View in network]] action.
5. **Change the scope.** Return to [[Map]] or [[List]], adjust the search or filters, and reopen [[Stats]].

- **Stats:** a summary of the records included in the current search and filter selection.

## Person profile

**Purpose:** show the complete record for one person.

A person profile brings together identity, photo, description, location, education, work, tags, Flemish and US connections, contact details, sources, and record history. Staff can also edit, update, print, collect, or delete the record when their role permits it.

1. **Review the main details.** Check the photo, name, verification status, headline, About, and Location sections.
2. **Review background information.** Check Education / Occupation, professional sectors, and tags.
3. **Review connections.** Open Flemish Connection and US Connection source links.
4. **Review contact details.** Open the source beside an email, LinkedIn profile, website, or other retained contact detail.
5. **Check the history.** Review Stats for the date added, who added it, how it was added, and the last update.
6. **Work with the profile.** Select [[Edit]], [[Update]], [[Add to Collection]], [[Print]], or [[Delete]] as needed.

- **Verification status:** shows whether the profile has completed the deeper source check and whether a staff member has reviewed it.

## Organization profile

**Purpose:** show the complete record for one organization.

An organization first appears in Verification with its evidence, as shown here for imec. After it is added to the network, its profile combines the name, type, locations, website, description, sectors, connections, related people, and retained sources.

1. **Review the evidence before adding.** In [[Verification]], check the organization’s description, scope, evidence, and source links.
2. **Add the reviewed organization.** After verification finishes, select [[Add]].
3. **Review the organization profile.** Check its name, type, location, website, sectors, About section, Flemish Connection, and US Locations.
4. **Open supporting information.** Select a retained source or a related person.
5. **Change or recheck the record.** Select [[Edit Organization]] and [[Save Changes]], or select [[Verify]] for a new source check.
6. **Use the organization.** Select [[Add to Collection]] or [[Visit Website]].

- **Organization profile:** the main directory page for a company, university, research institute, government body, or other organization.

## Collections

**Purpose:** organize selected people and organizations into working groups.

Collections are reusable lists for missions, events, outreach, or research. The overview shows each collection’s description, member count, and latest date.

1. **Open the overview.** Select [[Collections]].
2. **Create a collection.** Select [[New Collection]].
3. **Describe the group.** Enter a name and optional description, then save it.
4. **Open a collection.** Select its card or [[View Detail]].

- **Collection:** a saved group of network records. A record can belong to more than one collection.

## Collection detail

**Purpose:** manage the contents and notes of one collection.

This page shows the collection members and provides tools to edit the collection, add notes, remove members, export the list, and find relevant additions.

1. **Review the members.** Select a person or organization to open its profile.
2. **Change the collection.** Select [[Edit collection name/description]], then save the changes.
3. **Maintain the list.** Add notes or select [[Remove from collection]] beside a member.
4. **Download or remove the collection.** Select [[Export]] or [[Delete Collection]].
5. **Find additions.** Select [[Find Collection Suggestions]], review each result, and use [[Approve]], [[Reject]], or [[Undo]].
6. **Add reviewed suggestions.** Select [[Add Approved]]. Use [[Refresh]] for new suggestions or [[Reset]] to clear them.

- **Collection suggestion:** a person or organization proposed because it appears relevant to the collection’s subject or members.

## Grow / Import / Add Manually

**Purpose:** enter one person or organization that is not yet in the network.

The manual form is used when you already know the basic information. The new entry goes to Verification so its details and sources can be checked before it is added to the main directory.

1. **Open the form.** Select [[Grow]], [[Import]], and [[Add Manually]].
2. **Choose the record type.** Select [[People]] or [[Organizations]].
3. **Enter the known information.** Complete the identity, location, role or type, links, description, connections, sectors, and evidence fields that apply.
4. **Save a person.** Select the highlighted [[Create]] button below the person form.
5. **Save an organization.** Select the highlighted [[Create]] button below the organization form.
6. **Continue the process.** Open [[Verification]] and start the source check for the new entry.

- **Discovered record:** a person or organization that has been found or entered but has not yet been added to the main network.

## Grow / Import / Import File

**Purpose:** enter several people or organizations from one file.

File import is useful for an existing spreadsheet or contact list. You can use a template, match its columns to the platform, review errors, and send the valid rows to Verification.

1. **Open file import.** Select [[Grow]], [[Import]], and [[Import File]].
2. **Choose the record type.** Select [[People]] or [[Organizations]].
3. **Prepare the file.** Select [[Download CSV template]] or [[Download Excel template]] when needed.
4. **Upload the file.** Select the upload area and choose a CSV, TSV, TXT, XLS, or XLSX file.
5. **Match the columns.** On Map Columns, connect the file columns to platform fields and select [[Preview Import]].
6. **Review and create the entries.** On Confirm Import, check errors and duplicates, then select [[Create records]].

- **Column mapping:** the link between a column in the uploaded file and the matching field in the platform.

## Grow / Discovery

**Purpose:** search for new people and organizations that may belong in the network.

Discovery uses a written request to search selected web sources. It saves the names and initial evidence it finds so staff can decide which records should receive a deeper verification.

1. **Describe the search.** Enter a clear request in Discovery prompt.
2. **Start the search.** Select [[Run Discovery]].
3. **Use a suggested search.** Select [[Show suggestions]], review the proposals, and select [[Launch]] on a useful one.
4. **Follow the work.** Open [[Runs]] to see its progress, prompt, result, and errors.
5. **Review the results.** Open [[Verification]] and use the Discovered tab.

- **Discovery:** the first search that finds possible additions and retains initial evidence for review.

## Grow / Runs

**Purpose:** show the history and current status of automated work.

Runs lists every Discovery and Verification job. It shows who started it, when it ran, what it found, its cost, and any operational error.

1. **Open the history.** Select [[Grow]] and [[Runs]].
2. **Read the summary.** Review status, type, Started by, prompt, start time, duration, outcome, and cost.
3. **Open the technical detail.** Select [[View details]] for individual steps and error information.
4. **Update the list.** Select [[Refresh]].
5. **Stop active work.** Select [[Cancel]] on a queued or running job.

- **Run:** one recorded execution of Discovery or Verification, including its start time, result, and errors.

## Grow / Verification

**Purpose:** check a discovered record in detail before adding it to the network.

Verification searches for fuller and more current information, keeps the supporting sources, and presents the result for staff review. The workflow moves from Discovered to Queued, Being verified, Verified, and finally [[Add]].

1. **Choose what to check.** On the [[Discovered]] tab, review the summary, evidence, source count, and duplicate warning.
2. **Preview the expected profile.** Select the name or [[Preview profile]].
3. **Start verification.** Select [[Verify]] for one record or [[Verify all]] for the displayed group.
4. **Follow the status.** Use the workflow cards to see Queued for verification and Being verified. Select [[Retry verification]] after a failed check.
5. **Review the result.** Open the [[Verified]] tab and check the complete profile and its sources.
6. **Finish the review.** Select [[Add]], [[Reject]], or [[Merge]] when the record matches an existing profile.

- **Verified:** the deeper source search has finished and the result is ready for a staff decision. It is not added to the network until [[Add]] is selected.

## Grow / Maintenance

**Purpose:** keep network information and the automated maintenance work reliable over time.

Maintenance shows record age, background schedules, search-index work, stuck jobs, and sourced profile changes. From this page, staff can start maintenance work and decide which proposed changes belong in the network.

1. **Review record age.** Use [[Up to date]], [[Aging]], [[Stale]], or [[Outdated]] under Records Freshness.
2. **Start a maintenance cycle.** Select [[Run Housekeeping]] or the run button for Discovery or Verification.
3. **Set the schedules.** Choose [[Off]], [[Light]], [[Normal]], or [[Aggressive]] for each automated job.
4. **Manage the search index.** Review queued embedding work and select [[Drain now]] when work should start immediately.
5. **Handle stuck work.** Review Stuck Runs and select [[Cancel]] when a job should no longer continue.
6. **Review proposed changes.** Compare the current and proposed values and their sources, then select [[Approve]], [[Approve all]], [[Reject]], or [[Refresh]].

- **Update suggestion:** a sourced change proposed for a record that is already in the network.

## Settings / System

**Purpose:** monitor the services and usage behind the platform.

System shows API use, estimated cost, run-intensity definitions, service connections, and connectivity controls. Links to provider dashboards are available for configuration work.

1. **Review usage.** Check Gemini calls, Tavily calls, estimated cost, and the 14-day chart.
2. **Review run levels.** Read the Light, Normal, and Aggressive definitions.
3. **Check connections.** Review the status of Supabase, Gemini, Tavily, and Vercel.
4. **Test the database.** Select [[Test Supabase]].
5. **Reload the page.** Select [[Refresh]].
6. **Open provider settings.** Select [[Add backend API key]] or [[Manage frontend variables]].

- **Run intensity:** the preset that defines how often automated work runs and how many records it checks.

## Settings / Access

**Purpose:** control who can use the workspace and what they can do.

This admin page lists active staff accounts and their roles. Admins can add access, change a name or role, and remove access.

1. **Add a staff account.** Enter the email, optional full name, and role, then select [[Add Access]].
2. **Review the account list.** Check email, name, role, status, and last sign-in.
3. **Change an account.** Edit the name or role and select [[Save]].
4. **Remove access.** Select [[Remove]].

- **Staff access:** permission for one email address to sign in to this workspace with a specific role.

## Settings / My Account

**Purpose:** manage your own staff name and password.

My Account shows your email and role and lets you update your display name or password. Email and role are managed separately by an administrator.

1. **Open your account.** Select your name or select [[Settings]] and [[My Account]].
2. **Change your name.** Edit Full name and select [[Save Changes]].
3. **Enter a new password.** Complete New password and Confirm password.
4. **Save the password.** Select [[Update Password]].

- **My Account:** the settings page for the staff member who is currently signed in.

## Sign in and password recovery

**Purpose:** start a staff session or regain access to an account.

The sign-in page accepts an email address that has access and its password. The same page can send a reset link when the password is no longer known.

1. **Sign in.** Enter the email address that has access and its password, then select [[Sign In]].
2. **Request a reset.** Enter that email address and select [[Reset Password]].
3. **Open the email.** Follow the reset link back to the platform.
4. **Set the new password.** Complete the password fields and select [[Update Password]].
5. **End a shared session.** Select the exit icon when finished.

- **Password reset:** a time-limited email link used to choose a new password for an existing staff account.
